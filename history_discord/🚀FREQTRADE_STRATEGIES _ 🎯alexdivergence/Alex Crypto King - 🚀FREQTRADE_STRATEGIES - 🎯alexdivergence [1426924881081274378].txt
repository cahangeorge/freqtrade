[01/09/2026 01:52] sera131
The "Time Travel" Bug (Lookahead Bias)
The core issue lies in divergence_finder_dataframe (Lines 1050-1051 and 1095-1098).
When the strategy detects a divergence at the current candle ( index ), it goes back and updates the record for the candle 30 periods ago :

```
 Inside divergence_finder_dataframe loop at 'index'
if index > 30:
    # Writes to the PAST (index - 30) based on CURRENT 
    (index) finding
    dataframe.loc[index-30, 
    "total_bearish_divergences_count"] += 1
```


[01/09/2026 01:52] sera131
- In Backtesting: The strategy processes the whole list. When it checks candle T , the value total_bearish_divergences_count has already been updated by the loop when it reached T + 30 . The strategy "sees" a divergence count that won't actually be detected until 30 candles in the future.
- In Live Trading: At candle T , the loop hasn't reached T + 30 yet. The count will be 0. The signal will effectively never trigger (or trigger 30 candles late, likely missing the move). 2. Pivot Point Lookahead
The pivot_points function (Line 1169) uses a window of 5 candles to confirm a high/low.

```
 Checks 5 candles ahead to confirm if current is a pivot
if len(last_values) >= window * 2 + 1:
    # ... logic confirming pivot ...
    pivot_points_highs[index - window] = ...
```
- A pivot at candle T is only confirmed at candle T + 5 .
- The strategy treats these pivots as if they were known immediately at T , adding another 5 candles of lookahead. 3. Entry Logic
The entry logic relies on this tainted data:

```
 Reads the 'future-leaked' count
```

**What’s wrong (the flaw):**

**Scenario:** A long position is opened at $100, price drops to $95 (−5%), and a strong bearish divergence appears (3+ indicators).

RSI = 35 (oversold, since price is falling)
Condition `rsi > 60` = **FALSE**
➡️ **The exit does NOT trigger**, even though the divergence is clearly signaling a bearish reversal.

**Why this logic is wrong:**

Bearish divergence forms when:

* Price makes **higher highs** (RSI is often in the **60–80** range)
* Indicators make **lower highs**

However, **by the time the exit is evaluated**, price may have already pulled back, and:

* RSI has dropped below 60
* While the bearish divergence is **already confirmed and still valid**

**Conclusion:**
Tying the exit condition to `RSI > 60` is a logical mistake.
RSI reflects the **current state**, whereas divergence is a **structural signal formed in the past**.
