[09/21/2024 15:01] anonymuis
Futures are different in 4 ways:
1) you don't own the coin you buy. You just buy a 'contract' with a promise you will sell them 'soon'.
2) you can get a long contract I'm which you buy first, with a promise to sell later. Or a short contract in which you sell first (go negative) but have to buy back later.
3) you can do leverage: if you have 10 dollar and you buy a coin long with 1 a price of 1 dollar. With a leverage of 1, you could buy 10 long contracts. But with a leverage of 10x,  you could buy 100 coins. But, if the price drops 10% (as in the price goes to 0,9, you own 100 coins, so you have a loss of 10 dollar=you total amount, your contract is being ended and your money is liquidates (you lose it).
4) if you hold a contacts for a long time, you have to pay a small fee of even receive a small fee. It depends if the contract price is higher or lower than the price of the real coin. But don't bother this for a while. It makes the concept of futures q bit more complicated.

Short = a contract on which you sell first, and have to buy back later.

[09/21/2024 15:20] anonymuis
Just to put some more info in your spinning head:
The price of a future can be a little bit higher or lower than the corresponding coin. If a lot of people think the price wil go up, they will all go long and the price of future contracts can go more up than the coin itself.

This gives 2 opportunities to traders:
1) you could buy the coin on spot and go short (sell) on the contract: Now it doesn't bother if the price of the coin goes up or down. Of you lose 10% on spot, you will profit 10% on short. But of the price of the coin and the future are getting back together, you could sell both (and go neutral again) and you could have a really small profit from the price difference on which you started. This could be good strategy, because it doesn't impose risks on price movement.

2) Also (and this was point 4 of above) every X hours (mostly every 4 hours). The exchanges looks at the price of the coin, the futures and all long and short contracts:
If the price of the future is higher that the coin, all people with active long contracts have to pay a very small amount to all people with short contracts.
So, the effect is; every 4 hours people are being rewarded for starting short contracts if the futures price is higher than the coin. This makes the price of the futures go down again, so it keeps more in line of the price of the coin. This is the reason that a future is almost always pretty much the same price as the coin.

But again, IT IS NO COIN! A contract is nothing. It's pure price speculation on the price of another object.
Still, there are MILLIONS put in future contracts. Many of those are loosing and many are winning a bit. It's fun to think of it that way: a lot of people putting money on contracts buying 'nothing', but only the obligation to sell 'nothing' on a later date! Even a comedian can't come up with such a joke.
